## Assumptions (FEAT-SRC-n)
- FEAT-SRC-1: Suppliers and requests are marked; MPNs, list entries and relations are not, and are read through the part's markings (and the supplier's). An entry whose supplier
  the reader cannot read is invisible and does not count in that reader's coverage.
- FEAT-SRC-2: "Foreign" is an explicit flag on the supplier (foreign ownership, control or foreign-person staff with access). A country other than USA forces it. The ITAR rule
  has no override, also not through an export authorization on the part (unlike INT-6 for recipients): a foreign supplier is never selected for or sent ITAR data. It is enforced in the services,
  in database triggers and at read time. EAR-marked parts are governed by the clearance profile only.
- FEAT-SRC-3: Statuses are prospective, approved, conditional, disapproved, obsolete. Approved and conditional come only from a review with signatures; approved needs one valid
  (unexpired, unrevoked) certification at proposal and at approval. Disapproved and obsolete are set directly with a reason; obsolete is final. A supplier in review is frozen.
- FEAT-SRC-4: Certification expiry notices at 60, 30 and 7 days and on expiry, once each, to the watchers of the supplier (the creator auto-watches). The supplier's status does not
  change by itself; an expired certification makes it a conditional source in coverage. Certification files are not stored with the supplier (certificates arrive with responses).
- FEAT-SRC-5: Contacts are personal data, visible to readers of the supplier, never in audit details, notifications or logs. Audit details of the module carry numbers, codes and counts only.
- FEAT-SRC-6: Quality references are an opaque kind and id. A Quality module may register an `IQualityReferenceResolver` to show a title and link; nothing is resolved by default.
- FEAT-SRC-7: One table holds AML (part x manufacturer part) and AVL (part x vendor, optionally one MPN). Entry status is preferred, approved, alternate or disapproved; approval
  state is Draft, UnderReview, Active, Withdrawn. An Active entry changes only by end date or withdrawal; any other change is a new entry. One open entry per part, revision scope, list, supplier and MPN.
  A scope is a revision letter that must exist; null covers every revision.
- FEAT-SRC-8: An entry can be approved only for a supplier that is approved or conditional (a negative `disapproved` entry needs no standing), re-checked against the part's current markings when it is
  submitted and when it is approved. The entry review uses the part's markings for who may review and sign.
- FEAT-SRC-9: Coverage: an entry counts when Active and inside its dates. Covered = an effective preferred or approved entry of an approved supplier without an expired certification.
  Conditional = only alternates, conditional suppliers or expired certification. A line needs a source when it is a leaf with source Buy (EBOM) or supply type Buy, not phantom, leaf (MBOM).
  The EBOM uses the revision of the first line of a part; MBOM lines carry no part revision, so revision-scoped entries do not count there. Lines the reader cannot read are left out
  and not counted; a part the sourcing read does not return is treated the same way.
- FEAT-SRC-10: Relations are alternate or replaces, need Modify on both parts and Read on both; a relation is listed only to readers of the other part; two parts cannot replace each other. No review.
- FEAT-SRC-11: A request fixes its parts and recipients at creation (cancel and create a new one to change them). Its markings must dominate every part. Issue needs at least one
  releasable recipient; other recipients are shown to the buyer as withheld, with the reason. Release is decided at every view, nothing is cached: profile, request markings, each
  part. A supplier with no profile, a disapproved or obsolete supplier, and a foreign supplier for ITAR data receive nothing.
- FEAT-SRC-12: Portal scope is the portal-user link only (administrators link a user who has signed in once and holds the supplier role; one supplier per user; users with internal roles
  cannot be linked and are refused by the portal). The supplier role has no `PlmAction`. Another supplier's request, a draft, a cancelled request and an unknown number are all "not found".
- FEAT-SRC-13: Responses are immutable; the latest counts, earlier ones stay. Responses are accepted until the request is closed (due dates are informational). At most 5 files of 25 MB,
  kinds response or certificate; names are reduced to a safe leaf; all go through the vault scan and one rejection rejects the whole response. Files are delivered as attachments of
  unknown type, unstamped (they are the supplier's data, not marked renditions).
- FEAT-SRC-14: The supplier signs in through the same OIDC; the dev realm has `sup-alpha` and `sup-bravo`. A supplier's home page goes to the portal and the shell shows only the portal link (no search, palette or bell).
- FEAT-SRC-15: `supplier` is an awareness kind (follow, comment, recent, favorite) and a workspace kind; the object-type CHECK constraints of `ws` and `ntf` are replaced by a pattern
  by migration 0020, found by their definition (other modules adding kinds may do the same; a later migration that re-lists kinds without `supplier` would break this).
- FEAT-SRC-16: Search prefix `s:` finds suppliers; the default search includes them. Palette: "Go to Suppliers".
- FEAT-SRC-17: The sourcing API is a separate OpenAPI document; internal and portal routes use different policies. File uploads in the UI go through the circuit; the REST response endpoint takes multipart form data.
- FEAT-SRC-18: Docker-less test runs: `KEEL_TEST_PG` ("Host=127.0.0.1;Port=...;Username=postgres") points the integration fixture at an existing PostgreSQL 17 bootstrapped with
  `deploy/compose/initdb/00-roles.sql`; unset, the fixture starts its container as before.

## Not built / not verified
- No notification to the buyer when a response arrives; no supplier self-service for certifications or company data; no price comparison across suppliers; no PDF stamping of supplier files.
- The browser end-to-end tests and the axe audit additions (`SourcingTests`, `SourcingSeed`, the sourcing targets in `AccessibilityTests`) compile but were not run: the Docker daemon was unresponsive for the whole session.
  Everything else (unit, integration including the SQL oracle, API through the real host, OpenAPI drift) ran against a local PostgreSQL 17 through `KEEL_TEST_PG`; tests that need the vault container could not run.
- Real malware scanning of portal uploads was exercised with a fake vault that rejects an EICAR payload, not with clamd.
